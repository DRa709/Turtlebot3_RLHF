# Random initial-state specification — $\nu_R$ (standalone package 1.0.1)

The executable sampler is `turtlebot3_drl_nav/initialization.py`; arena geometry
comes from `turtlebot3_drl_nav/geometry.py` parsing the same authenticated world
that Gazebo loads. Numerical values live only in
`config/common_environment.yaml`.

## Randomized state and fixed context

Every episode draws the robot start pose $s_0=(x,y,\psi)$. The goal remains
$(2,0)$, the map and physics remain unchanged, and both obstacles begin at their
world reset poses. Each obstacle receives a seeded direction and phase offset
for that episode. Thus the intervention is robot-start diversity plus a common,
recorded obstacle schedule—not goal or map randomization.

## Distribution

Let $B=[-2,2]\times[-2,2]$ and define the admissible region

$$
\mathcal A=\{(x,y)\in B:
d_{static}(x,y)\ge 0.30,
d_{corridor}(x,y)\ge 0.30,
\|(x,y)-(2,0)\|\ge0.50\}.
$$

`d_static` is Euclidean centre-to-surface distance from the four walls and two
rotated static boxes. `d_corridor` is distance from the full swept footprint of
each dynamic obstacle. The swept amplitude is
$0.22\,\mathrm{m/s}\times5\,\mathrm{s}=1.1\,\mathrm m$ along its motion axis.
The declared robot footprint radius is 0.12 m. The 0.30 m centre-to-surface
rule therefore implies at least 0.18 m body-surface clearance; equivalently,
the collision-only constraint obtained by inflating every obstacle by the
0.12 m footprint is strictly dominated by the declared 0.30 m rule.

$$
(x,y)\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(\mathcal A),\qquad
\psi\sim\operatorname{Uniform}[-\pi,\pi),
$$

with yaw independent of position. Implementation uses uniform proposals on
$B$ and deterministic rejection, which preserves uniformity over $\mathcal A$.
At most 10,000 rejected proposals are allowed; exhaustion is fatal and has no
fallback. A two-million-point deterministic Monte Carlo characterization using
NumPy seed 20260903 gives 1,019,658 acceptances (fraction 0.509829) and area
approximately 8.157264 m². Reproduce it with
`scripts/characterize_initialization.py`. The geometric minimum start clearance
is 0.30 m, so the declared law contains no near-penalty-band starts.

## Seed roles and index purity

| Identity | Role | Controlled value |
|---|---|---:|
| `world_seed` | Gazebo RNG | 7000 |
| `initialization_seed` | training start poses | 7001 |
| `dynamic_obstacle_seed` | training obstacle phases | 7002 |
| `evaluation_seed` | E1/E3 and frozen E2 generation | 9001 |

The learning seed affects the learner only. A 63-bit generator seed is the first
eight SHA-256 bytes of the ordered tuple `(role seed, stream tag, indices)`.
Therefore an episode draw does not depend on earlier rejection counts, and the
same role seeds give the same episode-indexed starts and obstacle phases across
all separate algorithm packages.

- training episode $e$: `train_start_pose,e` and `train_obstacle_phase,e`;
- E1 checkpoint index $k$, episode $i$: `E1_start_pose,k,i` and
  `E1_obstacle_phase,k,i`;
- E3: canonical anchor pose plus `E3_obstacle_phase,k,i`;
- E2: frozen scenario row with its recorded start-generator and obstacle-phase
  seeds.

Every requested training and E1 pose and every obstacle phase is regenerated by
the validator from these identities.

## Obstacle trajectory

For obstacle $j$ with sign $s_j\in\{-1,+1\}$ and phase
$\phi_j\in[0,5)$,

$$
v_j(t)=s_j(-1)^{\lfloor(t-t_0+\phi_j)/5\rfloor}0.22
$$

along its configured axis. The controller uses the ROS simulation clock, not
wall time. The environment installs a schedule while physics is paused, waits
for an acknowledgement, and independently compares both measured obstacle
positions with the analytic integral on every transition. Error above 0.05 m is
fatal. Obstacle odometry publishes at 50 Hz and controller commands at 100 Hz.

## Transactional realization

Every training and evaluation episode, including episode 1, follows this order:

1. publish zero robot velocity; request obstacle hold and await the matching
   episode-key acknowledgement;
2. call and await `/reset_world`;
3. call and await `/set_entity_state` for the requested robot pose; require
   `success=true`;
4. unpause, settle for 0.5 simulation seconds, publish zero velocity, call and
   await `/pause_physics`;
5. while paused, capture scan, robot odometry and both obstacle odometries that
   are newer than the teleport and no more than 0.075 simulation seconds old;
6. call and await `/get_entity_state` for the robot and both obstacles;
7. require requested-to-realized position/yaw errors no larger than 0.03,
   odometry-to-realized position no larger than 0.05 m and yaw no larger than
   0.03 rad; require realized and odometry positions in $\mathcal A$, initial
   scan clearance at least 0.30 m, obstacles within reset tolerance and no
   reset contact;
8. install the seeded obstacle schedule at the captured simulation time, await
   its acknowledgement, record the complete initialization block, and only then
   publish the first policy observation.

Service, acknowledgement, sensor and action timeouts are fatal. Failed
teleports, stale sensors or invalid realized poses never fall back to a default
spawn.

## Evaluation conditions

| Condition | Start | Obstacle phase | Schedule |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | new $\nu_R$ draw | evaluation seed, checkpoint, episode | 20 episodes at every training checkpoint |
| E2 | one of 100 frozen stratified scenarios | scenario row | separate jobs at tier-2 checkpoints |
| E3 | canonical anchor $(0,0,0)$ | evaluation seed, checkpoint, episode | 20 episodes in each tier-2 job |

The E2 file is a deterministic, frozen draw from $\nu_R$, stratified across
four start–goal distance bins and three absolute heading-error bins and labeled
by clearance and path obstruction. Ten rows are marked for later physical
evaluation. `scripts/make_evaluation_scenarios.py --check` must reproduce the
file byte-for-byte before submission.

The E3 anchor is outside $\operatorname{supp}(\nu_R)$ and is reported separately;
it is not sampled during random-start training.
